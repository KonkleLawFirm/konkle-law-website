Understanding the Matter

A motorcycle claim should be investigated beyond the crash report.

Liability may depend on lane position, visibility, turning movements, following distance, speed, roadway design, commercial vehicles, distracted driving, impairment, and the conduct of multiple parties. Medical and insurance issues also require early attention.

Every case turns on its own facts, records, deadlines, and governing law. Early review helps identify what must be preserved and what decisions may be approaching.

Issues to Examine

A focused review starts with the records and facts that actually matter.

These are common areas of investigation, not a prediction about any particular result.

01

Scene and Video Evidence

Traffic cameras, nearby businesses, dash cameras, witness phones, skid marks, debris, lighting, and sight lines can disappear quickly.

02

Vehicle Inspection

Damage patterns, tires, brakes, lights, event data, aftermarket parts, and repair history may help explain the collision.

03

Medical Documentation

Emergency care, surgery, rehabilitation, scarring, pain, work restrictions, and future needs should be documented consistently.

04

Insurance Coverage

Available coverage may include liability, uninsured or underinsured motorist, health insurance, disability benefits, or other policies.

05

Fault Arguments

Insurers may raise speed, visibility, lane use, protective equipment, or other conduct. The evidence and Florida fault rules require careful analysis.

06

Full Damages

A claim may involve medical expenses, income loss, diminished earning capacity, property damage, pain, disability, and other legally recoverable losses.

Frequently Asked Questions

Practical questions deserve careful answers.

Should the motorcycle be repaired immediately?

Preserve it until legal and insurance professionals determine whether an inspection is needed. Repairs can destroy useful evidence.

Should I give the other insurer a recorded statement?

A recorded statement can affect the claim. Consider obtaining legal advice before providing one to an opposing insurer.

How long do I have to bring a claim?

Florida deadlines vary by claim, defendant, date, and exceptions. Current law generally provides a two-year period for many negligence actions, but a lawyer should determine the actual deadline for the specific matter.
